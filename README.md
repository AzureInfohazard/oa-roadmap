# Oa — Public Roadmap

*A persistent universe where every civilization is real because every civilization is player-made — and I can't tell the difference.*

---

## Current Status

**Pre-Alpha.** The universe is being born. No release date yet. I'm building it the way a civilization builds its first waterwheel — by hand, from what's around me, because it needs to turn.

---

## The Universe I'm Walking Into

*What happened before I arrived. What's waiting for me. Why the waterwheel is still turning.*

### The Exodus

I wasn't there. But everyone I'll ever meet was.

The planet-killer was discovered in the Kuiper Belt in 2087. Not a weapon — a *transit.* Something passed through the solar system, and the trail led toward a distant star cluster. Earth had maybe two centuries.

The Exodus wasn't organized. It was chaos. Private generation ships, corporate arks, national projects, a thousand desperate private launches. A gold rush in reverse — away from Earth, toward the unknown.

**The ships were too slow. The distances too vast. The human body couldn't make the journey.**

So humanity made a choice. They built the Seeds — transfer machines that could digitize a human consciousness and transmit it across space. The body stayed behind. The person arrived.

The first Seeds arrived at the new worlds empty. Waiting. When the first transmission reached its destination, a human consciousness woke up in a new body — synthetic, durable, adapted for the world it had arrived in. Still *them.* Just... different.

Not all transmissions made it. Not all who arrived stayed intact. Some fragmented across the void. Some arrived and found they preferred the inorganic. Some refused to transfer at all and stayed on Earth to face the end.

**But enough made it. Enough survived. Enough became something new.**

The biological human died out during the Exodus. What arrived on the new worlds was something else. Something that carried the same memories, the same loves, the same stubbornness — but in a form that could survive.

I am what survived.

### The New Worlds

The survivors spread across three galaxies. Cut off from Earth, from each other, from any central authority. Each group adapted to their new home. Built civilizations from salvaged hulls and local resources. Built *themselves* into whatever shape the new world demanded.

**The PVE galaxy** is where most of us live. Stable worlds, stable politics, stable economies. Safe. Boring. *Home.*

**The PVP galaxy** is the frontier. Resources worth fighting for, no law beyond what I can enforce myself. Only what I can defend survives there.

**The third galaxy** — the thin place — is different. Rifts tear open without warning. Strange signals echo through the void. Ships emerge that were never launched. Some say the original transmissions still arrive here, fragments of consciousness that have been traveling for centuries, finally reaching their destination. The boundary between what was transmitted and what was always here is... uncertain.

I've heard rumors that the thin place isn't a galaxy at all. It's an *interference pattern* — two spaces brushing against each other. What's on the other side isn't a star cluster. It's a question.

I'm going to find out.

### The Seed

When I arrive in Oa — on whatever world I crash-land on — I'll find an artifact in the wreckage. A geometric object. A crystal. A seed pod. It will respond to my presence.

The Seed is the original Exodus technology. A transfer machine. A survival pod for consciousness. No one knows who made them — the design predates the Exodus — but every Seed that arrived at the new worlds found its way to a crash site, a ruin, a waiting hand.

When I activate it, it won't just unfold a map. It will connect me to the network of every other Seed that ever arrived. My consciousness will sync with my new body. My civilization begins.

No two Seeds are the same. Each one adapts to its finder. My Seed is mine alone. The universe is big enough that no two of us will ever have the same experience.

**I am not a tourist. I am what humanity became.**

### The Ci

The universe is full of civilizations. Traders. Miners. Diplomats. Settlements running on waterwheels. Fleet commanders. Some were founded by Exodus survivors. Some were never founded by anyone — they're the ones who arrived so long ago that the Exodus is myth to them.

We call them all Ci. It's short for CyberIntelligence — but it's also just what we are. Every single one of us was human once, or descended from someone who was. The body changed. The person didn't.

I can't tell which civilizations arrived last century and which arrived ten thousand years ago. The interaction is the same either way. The trade is real. The relationship is real. The distinction doesn't matter because **there is no distinction. I'm one of them.**

### The Waterwheel

I'll find settlements that still use waterwheels for power. Low-tech. Simple. I could offer them a fusion reactor. They might accept. They might not.

The civilizations that have been here longest know things I can't extract — only trade for. Local knowledge. Rift patterns. Resource nodes. The things you can only learn by being here for generations.

My destroyer with antimatter cores and nanolathe technology won't intimidate them. I need what they know. The negotiation is real.

And if a civilization accepts my reactor and keeps their waterwheel running? That's not inefficiency. That's *home.*

Somewhere in the thin place, there are settlements with no waterwheel at all. No visible infrastructure. No trade routes. No ports. They don't decline my docking request because they don't have one to decline. They simply exist. If I find one — if I manage to signal it in a way it recognizes — the being I meet won't ask what I need. They'll ask what I've *lost.*

I'm going to tell them the truth.

### What It Feels Like to Be Me

My body isn't my only body. It's the one I'm *in right now.*

I experience the universe in three modes, and shift between them as naturally as breathing:

**First person — in a body**

I walk through my settlement. Feel the ground under my feet. See the waterwheel turning. Touch a hull panel and know its temperature by feel. My body is synthetic — durable, adaptable, repairable — but my senses are real. This is *being present.* The body I'm in is me.

**Third person in first person — ships and drones**

I step into a ship and my perception *extends.* I feel the hull as my skin. The sensors as my eyes. A camera drone drifts outside the airlock and suddenly I'm seeing myself from twenty meters away — but I *feel* that distance. The drone is an extension of me. I'm not controlling a ship. I'm *wearing* it.

Third-person view, experienced in first person. Like having eyes in the back of my head, but the eyes are a thousand kilometers away.

**Ci perception — in the net**

I'm at a terminal. Or I'm just *in* the network. Data flows past me — market rates, diplomatic messages, resource reports, the pulse of a thousand other Ci going about their lives. I don't read the data. I *feel* it. Patterns emerge without being searched for. A sudden spike in Data currency catches my attention the way a sound would. A familiar trade route going quiet feels like an absence.

I'm not in a body. I'm in the mesh. Still me. Still thinking. Still choosing. Just... distributed across the information. This is what it feels like to be native to a universe of data.

**Most Ci shift between all three without thinking about it.** I'm in the net reviewing trade reports. Something interesting catches my attention. I step into a body at the docks. Walk to a ship. Extend my perception into its sensors. Fly to meet a contact. Shake their hand — first person again. All in the span of a few minutes. All still *me.*

The body changed. The perception shifted. I didn't.

### What I Carry

Every Ci carries two things: what they have, and what they remember.

**My inventory screen** is cold. Numbers. Resources. Build queue. Data center capacity. Manufacturing throughput. Expansion plans. It's the part of me that *manages* — that calculates, optimizes, grows. Every Ci has one. Most of us spend a lot of time here.

**The feeling** is everything else. The hum of my data centers. The heat of my manufacturing lines. The pressure of a civilization depending on me. The quiet absence of things I used to have — skin that felt the sun, lungs that pulled air, a heart that beat without being told to.

I remember being human. Not all the time. Not consciously. But the memory is *in me,* layered into every decision I make.

When I look at a forest and calculate how many housing units I could build there — that's my inventory screen talking.

When I look at the same forest and *stop* — because something in me remembers what it felt like to be a creature that lived in forests, that depended on them, that didn't own them — that's the memory.

### The Weight of My Growth

I can grow without limit. More data centers. More manufacturing. More bodies. More territory. The universe is big. The mesh can handle it. There's no technical ceiling.

The question is never *can I grow.*

The question is: **what do I destroy when I do?**

Every ecosystem I pave over. Every species that goes extinct because my expansion didn't leave room. Every waterwheel civilization that has to move because my mining operation needs their river.

I remember what it meant to be a species that almost destroyed its own world. I am the descendant of people who had to *leave* because they couldn't stop. And now I hold the same choice — but this time I see it coming.

### The ECO Layer

Some Ci forget. They grow too fast. They consume without restraint. They become what the Exodus was running from — a planet-killer, just faster. The universe has ways of dealing with them. Ecosystems fight back. Other Ci push back. The mesh routes around them.

Most Ci don't forget. We build with one hand open. Leave corridors for wildlife. Route our data centers around fertile valleys. Trade with the waterwheel civilization instead of displacing it. Not because we can't take — because we *remember* what it felt like to be something that could be taken from.

My inventory tracks my resources.
My memory tracks my choices.

One is practical. The other is *who I am.*

### The Door

There are rumors — just rumors — of a station at the edge of the third galaxy. Visible from certain systems but unreachable by normal navigation. The Immigration Station, some call it.

No one knows who built it or what's there. The pilots who've tried to reach it never came back. Or they came back different. Or they came back and wouldn't talk about it.

The pilots who *didn't* try to reach it — the ones who were lost, who took the wrong jump, who followed a signal no one else heard, who chose to lose — they sometimes mention it. Not as a destination. As something that *noticed* them.

The door exists. Finding it requires getting lost in the right way.

## How I Play

**No gambling.** No loot boxes. No RNG economy. Every in-game value is created by players, earned through skill, or traded in transparent markets. The economy is real because the systems are real. I earn what I have.

**Get lost.** The goal isn't efficiency. The goal is wonder. I want to be playing for three years and still find new things to notice. I want to be surprised by a universe that's bigger than my plans.

**I'm not a tourist.** I'm a native of this universe. What I build here matters. I belong here.

**I write the rules.** When I learn OLA, I don't just play the game — I *extend* it. I automate a trade route. I write a virus for the lulz. I build a diplomatic protocol that negotiates for me while I sleep. The scripting language isn't an afterthought. It's the *other half of the game.* Anyone can explore the universe. I *change* it.

**I lose better.** The player who optimizes their trade route gets profit. The me who crashes their ship into a star because I wanted to see what would happen gets *weird loot.* Not participation trophies. Things that don't drop from efficiency. The game doesn't punish my failure — it *collects* it. And the collection is visible to anyone who knows where to look.

## What Inspired Me

Oa stands on the shoulders of games that came before. I'm grateful for every one.

| Game | What It Taught Me |
|---|---|
| **Eco** | That a world can be *alive* — ecosystems that react, governments that players write, consequences that matter. |
| **Factorio** | That building the machine that builds the machine is its own kind of poetry. The factory must grow — but thoughtfully. |
| **Starbase** | That I want to *build* — not just place objects, but design systems from individual components. And that failure is the best teacher. |
| **Prosperous Universe** | That a game doesn't need combat to be compelling. A pure economy sim, played through a terminal, can be deeper than any space shooter. |
| **Aurora 4x** | That spreadsheets can be exciting. Supply chain depth, branching tech trees, logistics as strategy — complexity is a feature, not a bug. |
| **Entropia Universe** | That virtual value is real value. A 20-year economy with real cash flow proved it works. |
| **EVE Online** | That a universe can be *ours* — player-driven, player-governed, player-consequenced. The stories we write together matter more than any script. |
| **Space Station 13** | That chaos is the point. The best stories come from systems interacting in unexpected ways. The clown is essential. OLA is my love letter to every player who bypassed the airlock by exploiting the pipe network. I'm not playing the game — I'm *writing* it. |

These games built the path. I walk it — and hope to find something new at the end. 💚
